%-----------RESEARCH INTERESTS-----------
\section{Research Interests}

My research centers on large multimodal foundation models---vision--language, video--language, and, increasingly, agentic models that plan and act in digital and physical environments---and aims to make them sustainable to manage and reliable to deploy without costly retraining. I pursue this goal along three complementary directions: knowledge lifecycle management, faithful and efficient inference, and agentic and embodied AI.

\vspace{0.25cm}

\textbf{Knowledge Lifecycle Management: Acquisition, Retention, and Forgetting.}
I develop methods for selectively modifying the knowledge stored in large models, spanning machine unlearning and continual learning. Motivated by the observation that post-hoc removal often suppresses rather than truly erases knowledge, I am increasingly interested in model structures in which knowledge is localized and composable, so that editing becomes a matter of adding or detaching components rather than disentangling intertwined parameters.

\vspace{0.25cm}

\textbf{Faithful and Efficient Inference: Grounding and Compression.}
I work on making multimodal model outputs trustworthy and cheap to serve. On the faithfulness side, I study hallucination and other failures that emerge as inputs grow richer and more composite---spanning multiple images, modalities, or turns---asking how to measure whether an output is grounded in the intended evidence and how to correct it when it is not. On the efficiency side, I study how inference-time compression techniques that work well for simple inputs break down as inputs grow more complex, and how to design compression that scales with input structure rather than against it.

\vspace{0.25cm}

\textbf{Agentic and Embodied AI: Acting in Digital and Physical Environments.}
As foundation models move from passive perception to autonomous action, I am beginning to explore how they can plan, use tools, and act reliably---whether as digital agents navigating multi-step tasks or as embodied vision--language--action models operating in the physical world. This direction extends my work on knowledge lifecycle management and faithful inference to settings where models must update their skills over time and ground their decisions in real-world feedback rather than static labels.
